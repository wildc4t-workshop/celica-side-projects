# EPS Sources / Precedent

## Purpose

Preserve the external precedent used to frame the Celica EPS project without confusing general retrofit feasibility with a validated Celica-specific architecture.

## Current evidence state

### Confirmed general precedent

Toyota Prius column-EPS systems provide electric assist through a column-mounted torque sensor, motor, reduction mechanism, and EPS controller. General retrofit literature demonstrates that these units can be operated outside the original vehicle, but the vehicle-specific mechanical problem remains adapting the donor EPS shaft/column interfaces to the recipient vehicle safely and repeatably.

### Celica-specific precedent

A surviving forum discussion documents a 2002 seventh-generation Celica owner investigating a Corolla electric-power-steering column conversion. This supports the feasibility of adapting a Toyota column-EPS assembly into the chassis family, but it is not yet sufficient to define the user's exact mechanical architecture.

The previously remembered example of a **Prius EPS installed in a seventh-generation Celica** has not yet been reliably re-located. Treat that specific example as **unverified/anecdotal** until the original post or equivalent evidence is recovered.

## Research rule

External swaps are useful for:

- confirming packaging possibilities;
- identifying donor spline standards/couplers;
- understanding standalone EPS electrical behavior;
- revealing failure modes and ugly fabrication approaches to avoid.

They do **not** automatically justify copying a cut-and-weld steering-shaft solution.

The Celica project should use precedent to reduce uncertainty while still producing a drawing-controlled, inspectable mechanical interface.
